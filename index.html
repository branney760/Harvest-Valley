Sunlight to Sugar

A vineyard photosynthesis investigation

Connect the biology

Light-dependent reactions transform light energy into chemical energy carriers and release oxygen from water. The Calvin cycle uses those carriers to support carbon fixation. Carbon in newly made carbohydrates comes from atmospheric CO₂. Some carbohydrates support growth or are transported to developing grapes; leaf gas exchange alone does not measure fruit sugar.

What does this model include?

A saturating response to light, a broad temperature response, reduced carbon uptake under water stress, and respiration that increases with temperature over the model’s limited range. Water availability controls a simplified CO₂ access factor representing water-related stomatal limitation. This factor is not a measurement of stomatal aperture; light responses of stomata are not separately modeled. Opening stomata allows CO₂ entry but also allows water loss.

Temperature affects multiple processes. A decline here does not demonstrate enzyme denaturation. The model does not simulate irreversible injury, acclimation, photorespiration as a separate flux, canopy shading, growth, soil types, varieties, pests, or fruit ripening. Atmospheric CO₂, leaf area, nutrients and measurement interval are held constant. Deterministic repeat runs are not independent biological replicates.

The 25 °C peak in the gross-rate equation is an instructional parameter, not a universal optimum for grapevines. Net-rate peaks may differ because respiration is subtracted.

Sources and model equations

Biological background: OpenStax Concepts of Biology, 5.1; Chapter 5 summary; Biology 2e, 30.5.

The equations are purpose-built teaching assumptions, not fitted research models. Let L = light, T = temperature and W = water index. Water-related CO₂ access factor S = clamp((W − 15) / 65, 0.08, 1). Gross rate = 125 × L/(L + 25) × exp(−((T − 25)/16)²) × S. Respiration = 6 × 2^((T − 25)/10). Net uptake = gross − respiration. All outputs are relative rates, not physical gas-exchange measurements.
